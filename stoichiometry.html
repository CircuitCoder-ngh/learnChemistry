Stoichiometry

A balanced equation is a recipe. Stoichiometry is using that recipe to work out how much you need and how much you get.

  • Read the coefficients of a balanced equation as a mole ratio
  • Walk the map from grams to moles to moles to grams
  • Solve mass, mole and particle problems with the units canceling
  • Say why grams only add up in total, never substance by substance

A balanced equation is a recipe

The word looks scary. Say it "stoy-kee-OM-eh-tree". It just means measuring how much of each thing goes into a reaction and how much comes out.

You already know how to do it, because you can follow a recipe. Start with a sandwich.

The coefficients in a balanced equation are a mole ratio. That one idea runs this whole lesson.

The number in front of a formula is called a coefficient. A coefficient you cannot see is a 1. Nobody writes it, but it is there, like the bread you forgot to buy.

Run the recipe yourself

Pick a reaction and choose how much to start with. Every atom on the left has to land somewhere on the right, so you can count them. Can you find a reaction where the total number of molecules goes down? Then find one where it goes up.

Why you cannot use grams directly

Here is the most common mistake in this whole topic. Students see "2 to 1" in an equation and treat it as 2 grams to 1 gram. Watch what goes wrong.

Exam trap: coefficients are not grams

In $\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$, the mole ratio of $\ce{H2}$ to $\ce{O2}$ is 2 to 1. The mass ratio is about 1 to 8. Coefficients count moles. Only use them on moles. If a problem gives grams, change to moles first, every single time.

Now compare moles and grams side by side. Look at the two rows of bars below and ask which row stays equal from before to after.

Exam trap: what is conserved?

Total mass stays the same, because atoms stay the same. Total moles of molecules can change, because atoms regroup into more or fewer molecules. And the grams of any one substance change, of course. That is what a reaction does.

The map: down, across, up

Almost every stoichiometry problem is the same trip with different numbers. You know grams of one thing and you want grams of another. The recipe only speaks moles, so the only way across is through the mole hole.

The whole trip on one line

$$\begin{aligned} \text{g A} &\times \frac{1\text{ mol A}}{\text{molar mass A}} & &\text{down} \\ &\times \frac{b\text{ mol B}}{a\text{ mol A}} & &\text{across} \\ &\times \frac{\text{molar mass B}}{1\text{ mol B}} = \text{g B} & &\text{up} \end{aligned}$$
$a$, $b$
the coefficients of A and B in the balanced equation

Worked example: grams to grams

Iron is made from its ore by the reaction $\ce{Fe2O3 + 3CO -> 2Fe + 3CO2}$. How many grams of iron can you get from 80.0 g of $\ce{Fe2O3}$?

  1. Check the equation is balanced. Left: 2 Fe, 3 C, 6 O. Right: 2 Fe, 3 C, 6 O. Good.
  2. Find the molar masses. $\ce{Fe2O3}$: $2(55.85) + 3(16.00) = 159.70\text{ g/mol}$. $\ce{Fe}$: $55.85\text{ g/mol}$.
  3. Go down, from grams to moles. $80.0\ \cancel{\text{g Fe}_2\text{O}_3} \times \dfrac{1\text{ mol Fe}_2\text{O}_3}{159.70\ \cancel{\text{g Fe}_2\text{O}_3}} = 0.501\text{ mol Fe}_2\text{O}_3$.
  4. Go across with the mole ratio. The coefficients say 2 Fe for every 1 $\ce{Fe2O3}$. $0.501\ \cancel{\text{mol Fe}_2\text{O}_3} \times \dfrac{2\text{ mol Fe}}{1\ \cancel{\text{mol Fe}_2\text{O}_3}} = 1.002\text{ mol Fe}$.
  5. Go up, from moles to grams. $1.002\ \cancel{\text{mol Fe}} \times \dfrac{55.85\text{ g Fe}}{1\ \cancel{\text{mol Fe}}} = $ $56.0\text{ g Fe}$. Three significant figures, because 80.0 has three.

Walk it yourself

Now you drive. At each room, pick the conversion factor that lets the old unit cancel. Moe only moves when you pick the right one. Wrong picks are fine, and you will see why they fail.

Shorter and longer trips

Not every problem uses the whole map. If the problem starts in moles, you begin in the tunnel and skip the first leg. If it wants particles, you leave through a side room. The mole ratio in the middle is always there.

Worked example: moles to grams

Propane burns by $\ce{C3H8 + 5O2 -> 3CO2 + 4H2O}$. What mass of oxygen does 0.250 mol of propane need?

  1. You are already in the mole tunnel, so skip the first leg.
  2. Go across. The coefficients say 5 $\ce{O2}$ for every 1 $\ce{C3H8}$. $0.250\ \cancel{\text{mol C}_3\text{H}_8} \times \dfrac{5\text{ mol O}_2}{1\ \cancel{\text{mol C}_3\text{H}_8}} = 1.25\text{ mol O}_2$.
  3. Go up. $1.25\ \cancel{\text{mol O}_2} \times \dfrac{32.00\text{ g O}_2}{1\ \cancel{\text{mol O}_2}} = $ $40.0\text{ g O}_2$.

Worked example: grams to molecules

How many molecules of $\ce{CO2}$ form when 5.00 g of propane burns?

  1. Molar mass of $\ce{C3H8}$: $3(12.011) + 8(1.008) = 44.10\text{ g/mol}$.
  2. Go down. $5.00\ \cancel{\text{g C}_3\text{H}_8} \times \dfrac{1\text{ mol C}_3\text{H}_8}{44.10\ \cancel{\text{g C}_3\text{H}_8}} = 0.1134\text{ mol C}_3\text{H}_8$.
  3. Go across. 3 $\ce{CO2}$ for every 1 $\ce{C3H8}$. $0.1134\ \cancel{\text{mol C}_3\text{H}_8} \times \dfrac{3\text{ mol CO}_2}{1\ \cancel{\text{mol C}_3\text{H}_8}} = 0.3402\text{ mol CO}_2$.
  4. Leave through the side room. $0.3402\ \cancel{\text{mol CO}_2} \times \dfrac{6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ molecules}}{1\ \cancel{\text{mol CO}_2}} = $ $2.05 \times 10^{23}$ molecules.

Keep a few extra digits in the middle of a problem and round only at the end. Rounding at every step is how right methods get wrong answers. Worse, they get marked wrong by a computer that has no sympathy.

Pocket card

  • Coefficients are a mole ratio. They count moles, never grams.
  • Check the equation is balanced before you use it.
  • The map: down (divide by molar mass of A), across (multiply by the mole ratio), up (multiply by molar mass of B).
  • Write the mole ratio with what you want on top and what you know on the bottom, so the old unit cancels.
  • For particles, leave the mole tunnel by multiplying by $6.022 \times 10^{23}$.
  • Total mass is conserved. The grams of one substance are not, and total moles need not be.
