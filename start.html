What is chemistry?

Welcome to the hole. My name is Moe. I am a mole, I am a chemist, and I have heard all the jokes. Press Next whenever you are ready.

Chemistry is the science of what matter is made of and how it changes.

Three ways of seeing

Here is the secret skill of every good chemist. They look at one thing and see it three ways at once. Beginners get lost because nobody tells them which view they are in. You will always know.

Now you drive

Pick an event. Drag the slider and all three views change together. Then choose something to follow and watch it light up in the particles and in the symbols. Ask yourself: can you see a single atom from the left side of the picture?

Every fact in chemistry lives at three levels at once: what you can see, what the particles do, and how we write it.

Exam trap: particles are not tiny pieces of the big stuff

One water molecule is not wet. One copper atom is not orange. When a gas warms up, its molecules do not swell. They move faster. Properties like color, wetness and boiling belong to huge crowds of particles, not to one particle. Do not mix up the views.

Reading the symbols

The symbolic view is a tiny language, and you already know most of it. Each part of a formula tells you something about the particles.

How to read a formula

$$\textcolor{#D9127F}{3}\,\mathrm{H}_{\textcolor{#0E7FC0}{2}}\mathrm{O}\,\textcolor{#1E9E6A}{(l)}$$
Coefficient 3
How many whole molecules: 3 of them.
Subscript 2
How many of that atom in one molecule: 2 H. No number means 1, so 1 O.
State $(l)$
$(s)$ solid, $(l)$ liquid, $(g)$ gas, $(aq)$ dissolved in water.

Worked example: count the atoms

How many hydrogen atoms are in $\ce{3H2O}$?

  1. Read the subscript. One $\ce{H2O}$ molecule has 2 H atoms.
  2. Read the coefficient. There are 3 molecules.
  3. Multiply: $3 \text{ molecules} \times \dfrac{2 \text{ H atoms}}{1 \text{ molecule}} = $ 6 H atoms.

How small is small?

"Small" stops meaning anything down here, so let us measure it. This zoom is drawn at true scale. Every step of ten on the slider makes everything ten times bigger. Check the scale bar in the corner as you go.

ThingRough widthIn meters
A person (height)1.7 m$1.7 \times 10^{0}$ m

The atom is tens of thousands of times wider than its nucleus, yet nearly all the mass is in that speck. All of chemistry happens out in the electron cloud. The nucleus just sits there, being heavy. I respect that.

Exam trap: each power of ten is a multiply, not a step

$10^{-9}$ m is not "a bit smaller" than $10^{-6}$ m. It is 1,000 times smaller, because $10^{-6} \div 10^{-9} = 10^{3}$. To compare two powers of ten, subtract the exponents.

Pocket card

  • Chemistry is the science of what matter is made of and how it changes.
  • Matter is anything with mass that takes up space.
  • Three views: macroscopic (what you see), particle or submicroscopic (what atoms do), symbolic (how we write it).
  • In $\ce{3H2O(l)}$, the coefficient 3 counts molecules, the subscript 2 counts H atoms per molecule, and $(l)$ gives the state.
  • An atom is about $10^{-10}$ m wide. Its nucleus is about $10^{-15}$ m.
