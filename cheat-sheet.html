Equations: energy and heat

Learn it in , ,

Heat, no phase change$q = mc\Delta T$
Calorimeter$q = C\Delta T$, with $q_\text{system} = -q_\text{surroundings}$
Enthalpy per mole$\Delta H = \dfrac{q_\text{rxn}}{n}$ at constant pressure
First law$\Delta E = q + w$, with $w = -P\Delta V$
Hess's lawReverse a reaction: flip the sign of $\Delta H$. Multiply it: multiply $\Delta H$. Add them up.
From formation$\Delta H^\circ = \sum n\Delta H^\circ_f(\text{products}) - \sum n\Delta H^\circ_f(\text{reactants})$

Balancing and combustion

Learn it in , ,

  1. Write correct formulas. Only change coefficients, never subscripts.
  2. Balance a metal or the most complex molecule first.
  3. Keep polyatomic ions together as one unit.
  4. Leave H and O, and any lone element, for last.
  5. Clear fractions, then count every atom on both sides.

Combustion: C, then H, then O last

$\ce{C_xH_y} + \left(x + \tfrac{y}{4}\right)\ce{O2} \rightarrow x\,\ce{CO2} + \tfrac{y}{2}\,\ce{H2O}$

$\ce{C_xH_yO_z} + \left(x + \tfrac{y}{4} - \tfrac{z}{2}\right)\ce{O2} \rightarrow x\,\ce{CO2} + \tfrac{y}{2}\,\ce{H2O}$

$\ce{2C2H6 + 7O2} \rightarrow \ce{4CO2 + 6H2O}$

If $\ce{O2}$ needs a fraction ($\tfrac72$), double everything. Not enough oxygen: incomplete combustion makes CO and soot, such as $\ce{2CH4 + 3O2} \rightarrow \ce{2CO + 4H2O}$. Alkanes: $\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}}$.

Activity series

Learn it in

A metal displaces any metal ion below it. Most active at the top.

  1. Li K Ba Sr Ca Nareact with cold water
  2. Mg Al Mn Zn Cr Fe Cdreact with steam or acids
  3. Co Ni Sn Pbreact with acids
  4. $\ce{H2}$the dividing line
  5. Cu Hg Ag Pt Auwill not displace $\ce{H2}$ from acids

Halogens: $\ce{F2} \gt \ce{Cl2} \gt \ce{Br2} \gt \ce{I2}$. Each one displaces the halide ions below it. Exact order varies a little between textbooks.

Oxidation number rules

Learn it in

  1. A free element is 0: $\ce{Na}$, $\ce{O2}$, $\ce{S8}$.
  2. A monatomic ion equals its charge: $\ce{Fe^3+}$ is +3.
  3. All the numbers add up to the charge: 0 for a compound, or the ion's charge.
  4. Group 1 metals are +1, group 2 metals are +2, and aluminum is +3.
  5. Fluorine is −1.
  6. Hydrogen is +1 with nonmetals, −1 with metals, as in $\ce{NaH}$.
  7. Oxygen is −2, except −1 in peroxides ($\ce{H2O2}$).
  8. Other halogens are −1, unless bonded to O or a lighter halogen.

If rules clash, the higher rule wins. Mn in $\ce{KMnO4}$: $+1 + x + 4(-2) = 0$, so $x = +7$. OIL RIG: oxidation is loss, reduction is gain, of electrons.
